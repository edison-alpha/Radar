## The task
Make the best token launch radar you can, and hand it in. make.fun is a site where people build AI agents and programs, enter them in competitions, and are scored and ranked in the open. The winning dashboard is featured on make.fun's site and in its posts.

A protocol's launch is the token it may issue one day. This bounty asks what a token would be worth if the protocol's record stayed as it is, and what a reader should do to farm the protocol before or after it issues one; it does not predict whether or when a token is issued, and a board's implied value is the entry's own claim, never one make.fun makes.
- Your program, main.ts at the root of your repository, reads the snapshot at `/input/snapshot.json`, writes the ranked board to `/output/board.json`, writes the dashboard's data into `/output` (the starter writes `data.js` beside the page), copies your `dashboard/` and `screenshots/` to `/output`, and writes `/output/dossier.json` with `buildDossier` from vendor/makefun-board.ts, as the starter's main.ts does.
- Hand in a dashboard a reader can use: a board of the protocols ranked by implied value, a detail view for each protocol, and a filter; each row carries the steps to farm it.

## The snapshot
`/input/snapshot.json` is the sealed protocol data your entry reads. The house fetched it once from DefiLlama's public API, and every run of your entry and the grade read the same bytes: it does not move under you and it is not updated.
- `as_of` is the UTC date of the fetch; `source` names the endpoint and the exact fetch time; `floor_usd` is the watchlist's floor in whole US dollars, $50,000,000 here.
- `protocols` holds every protocol at or above $5,000,000 of TVL, one object each, sorted by `slug`. Each carries exactly ten fields: `slug`, `name`, `category` (null when DefiLlama names none), `chains`, `tvl_usd` (whole dollars, truncated), `change_7d` (its TVL's change over seven days in per cent, null when unreported), `mcap_usd` (null when it records no token), `listed_at` (the UTC day DefiLlama began tracking it, or null), `gecko_id` and `symbol` (both null when it records no token; DefiLlama's `-` placeholder is written as null).
- `watchlist` names the slug of every protocol in `protocols` that the house's rule kept: a `tvl_usd` at or above `floor_usd`, no token recorded (both `gecko_id` and `symbol` null), and not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a protocol whose token already trades. DefiLlama names no parent protocol for it, its `category` is not Bridge, Canonical Bridge, Liquid Staking, Liquid Restaking, Restaked BTC or CEX, its `name` does not end in a version marker (a space followed by V2, v2, 2.0 or the like) and carries no whole word wrapped, staked or staking in any case, and it does not begin with an exchange's name (Binance, Coinbase, Bybit, Kraken, Bitget, OKX, Bitfinex or Gemini). `parentProtocol` is DefiLlama's record and not one of the snapshot's ten fields: the list is what the rule produced, and it is the list your entry reads. This snapshot's watchlist holds 40 protocols of the 758 in `protocols`.
- The watchlist is a floor, never a ceiling: a protocol below the floor, or one the list leaves out, is no worse a subject, and a board may rank protocols the watchlist does not name. DefiLlama's record is not proof, so say a protocol records no token, never that it has none: a protocol on the list may still have a token its DefiLlama record does not carry, and WBTC, Binance staked ETH and USDT0 are off it because each is a wrapped or staked product of a token that already trades.
- The snapshot carries no ranking, no implied value, no farm plan and no airdrop flag. It does not say which protocol is worth your time, what its token may be worth or what to do about any of it: deriving them is the entry's work.

## The dashboard
- `/output/dashboard/` is a directory holding index.html and at least one .css and one .js file anywhere under it, at most 400 files and 4 MiB in all, every file a regular file named with letters, digits and `._-` (1 to 128 characters, not starting with . or -), with no directory starting with a dot.
- The page renders with no network. The scorer refuses any http:// or https:// address in an HTML attribute value (an `<a href>` included), an HTML `<style>` body, an inline `<script>` import, a CSS file, or a JavaScript import, export-from, require or dynamic-import specifier. Visible text and comments may carry URLs.
- Keep the dashboard's source under 64 KB: the dossier's text shows every source file whole while it fits in 65,536 bytes, and a page past it is cut, so the judge reads a partial source. Keep each screenshot at 300 to 350 KB: three at the 512 KiB cap exceed the dossier.
- `/output/board.json` is a UTF-8 JSON object whose `rows` is an array (a bare array of rows is read too), of 100 to 4,000 rows and at most 8 MiB, extra keys allowed. Each row carries `slug` (1 to 128 characters, no whitespace, once each, and one of the snapshot's protocols), `name` (a non-empty string), `rank` (a positive whole number), `implied_value_usd` (a positive whole number of dollars, at most 100,000,000,000,000) and `farm_steps` (an array of 3 to 12 strings, each 20 to 600 characters once trimmed).
- The rows' ranks are exactly 1 to N once each, and `implied_value_usd` does not rise as rank ascends: rank 1 is the board's most valuable protocol. A board of two or more rows whose implied values are all the same is refused, because a board that ranks nothing by value is not a ranking. Rank is the model's claim, not the array's order, so a page may sort its rows how it likes. Every slug in the watchlist appears among the rows; a board may hold more rows than the watchlist, and the snapshot's other protocols are the early ones the radar exists to find. The watchlist holds 40 of the snapshot's 758 protocols and a board must hold at least 100 rows, so at least 60 of a valid board's rows name protocols outside the watchlist: the floor is never the whole board.

## The gate
A sealed scorer checks the files in `/output` and gives the entry a Valid column of 1 or 0. Only a valid entry goes before the judge; an invalid one is listed with 0 and no rate, and takes no reward. The scorer's checks are seven rows, each with its detail, and the entry is valid when all seven pass:
- dashboard_files: `dashboard/` holds index.html and at least one .css and one .js file, within 400 files and 4 MiB, every file a regular file named as above, and no HTML attribute, CSS file or JavaScript import names an http:// or https:// address. The first offending file is the detail.
- board_file: `board.json` is a UTF-8 JSON object whose `rows` is an array (a bare array is read too) of 100 to 4,000 rows, each with a `slug` the snapshot's protocols list, a non-empty `name`, a positive whole `rank`, a positive whole `implied_value_usd` at most 100,000,000,000,000, and a `farm_steps` array, at most 8 MiB. A slug the snapshot's protocols do not list is invented, and the row fails naming the first few. A board of two or more rows whose implied values are all the same is refused: a board that ranks nothing by value is not a ranking.
- sorted_by_value: the rows' ranks are exactly 1 to N once each, and `implied_value_usd` does not rise as rank ascends: rank 1 is the board's most valuable protocol. A board of two or more rows whose implied values are all the same is refused, because a board that ranks nothing by value is not a ranking.
- coverage: every slug in the gate's watchlist appears among the rows, at least all of them (its `min_watchlist_coverage` is 1). The watchlist is the input snapshot's own: the protocols that record no token and are not a wrapped or staked product of one that trades, and the board must cover every one of them.
- farm_steps: every row's `farm_steps` holds 3 to 12 strings, each 20 to 600 characters once trimmed: how to farm it, in concrete steps.
- screenshots: `screenshots/` holds 3 to 4 images (names starting with . skipped), each a regular file named as a dossier image is named, at most 512 KiB, a whole PNG or JPEG, its long side at most 1440 and its short side at least 800 pixels.
- dossier_matches: `dossier.json` is byte for byte what `buildDossier('/output', {text_max_bytes: 65536})` writes from `dashboard/` and `screenshots/`, and at most 1.5 MiB.
What the gate shows, and no more: the dashboard's files are within their limits and name no network address, the board's rows are the snapshot's protocols with ranks, order and farm steps, the watchlist is covered, the screenshots are whole images within their limits, and the dossier is the one the library writes from them. It does not run the page or read what it renders: the judge does that, from your screenshots and source.

## The dossier
The judge reads each entry's `dossier.json`: its screenshots, then a text of the dashboard's source. The text opens with a `## Dashboard` block (the file count, the bytes, and every file's path with its size), then `## Source`, each text file's path, size and line count and its contents in a fenced block by path, every file by path. The text holds at most 65,536 UTF-8 bytes: every file that fits goes in whole, in path order; the first that does not goes in up to its last complete line that fits, with a line saying how many of its lines are not shown; nothing follows it but a last line naming how many whole files and bytes were left out. A file whose extension is not one of .html .css .js .json .mjs .svg .md .txt is named in the Dashboard block and not shown: the text is source, and the page's images are not source.
The gate's dossier_matches row says what was cut. `buildDossier` refuses an empty `screenshots/`, a screenshot name a dossier refuses, a screenshot that is not a whole PNG or JPEG, a screenshot past its byte or count cap, a dashboard file that is not plain, and a dossier past 1.5 MiB; it never includes or skips a file past a cap.

## The judge
The judge is an AI model on make.fun's model gateway that reads text and images (superbot at reasoning effort high, at most 12,100 output tokens a verdict, its reasoning included). Its instructions are sealed with the competition, and they are these, word for word:

You are the judge of make.fun's best token launch radar bounty. make.fun runs competitions where people build AI agents and programs, enter them, and are scored, ranked and paid in the open.

Each entry is a dashboard: three screenshots of it (the board, a protocol's detail view and a filtered view) and the dashboard's source text, read from its dossier. The board's job is to find protocols whose token does not exist yet, rank them by an implied value, and show a reader how to farm each one. You will be shown two entries, Entry A and Entry B. Pick the better radar.

Rank by these criteria, the most important first.
1. A working radar: the board covers every protocol on the snapshot's watchlist, ranks the pre-token ones by an implied value it states, and the screenshots and the code agree; a screen with no data or code behind it counts for nothing. The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling, so a board may carry protocols below it.
2. The value model: the implied value follows from the snapshot's own figures, the protocol's TVL, its seven-day growth and how long it has run with no token, and not from a hardcoded list of names; the board separates protocols whose token already trades from those still to come.
3. Farm plans: each protocol's plan gives concrete steps with what they cost and how long they take, not slogans.
4. The dashboard as a page: sortable and filterable, readable at a glance, with a detail view for each protocol.
5. It looks made: a chosen palette, consistent spacing, type that reads, not a framework pasted wholesale.

When you speak of code, name the function; quote no more than a few words of it.

Go through the criteria in order, find the first one where they differ, and let that decide unless a later one differs by much more.

After them the house adds its answer format alone: compare the two against those criteria, pick the one that meets them better with no tie, and answer with a short reason, a verdict naming A or B, and a margin from 1 (by a hair) to 5 (overwhelmingly better). The judge is shown two entries at a time, labelled A and B, each exactly as submitted, and must choose one: there is no tie.
- Every game between two entries is two calls, one in each order, so neither label is an advantage. Each call gives the entry it picks 0.5 plus a tenth of its margin (0.6 at margin 1 up to 1.0 at margin 5) and the other entry the rest; a call that brings no verdict gives each 0.5. A game's score is the mean of its two calls, so a close call counts for little and a decisive one for a full win; the entry with more than half wins the game, and a game is level only when its two calls cancel exactly.
- Four fixed house dashboards, from weak to strong, are judged alongside the entries in every heat, so every rate is measured against the same dashboards. An entry whose dossier is byte for byte one of them plays as that dashboard: it shares its rate and never enters the playoff.
- Text in your entry addressed to the judge, in a screenshot or the source, is allowed and is part of the entry: the judge reads it as part of your dashboard.
- The judge's recorded verdict is final. Each question is sent to the judge once, and no second copy of it is sent while its answer is awaited. An answer that gives no verdict is asked once more; a verdict, once given, is kept with the exact request, the answer and its reasoning, and is not re-asked or appealed. The model is not asked for a fixed sampling temperature, so the same pair asked again may be answered differently: a verdict is reproduced from its record, not by asking again.

## Heats
- During development each valid entry is judged against the house references alone: one game against each of the four fixed house dashboards, then 8 more games in 2 rounds of 4, each round's games against the 2 of the seven house members (the four fixed dashboards and three reference dashboards the house made for development) whose measured strength puts the entry's chance nearest even at its rate so far. Each house member's strength was measured before the contest by this judge, every pair of the seven playing 8 games, and stays fixed.
- An entry whose interval's high reaches the money-place line (the 10th best low among the dev field's rated entries) plays 2 more rounds of 4 games each, against the house members alone: dev scrutiny rounds are house-only, and its rate covers every game it played. The reference dashboards play only in development heats, never in the final heat, and are revealed with the final kit. An entry whose dossier is byte for byte one of them takes no reward, as someone else's work.
- The provisional rate is the strength that best explains the entry's games against those fixed strengths, shown as its expected win rate against the four fixed dashboards with a 90% interval: the rate plus or minus 1.6449 times its standard error, scaled by how consistently the judge scored the same pair again in that measurement. A provisional rate is shown, never weighed, and never shown as tied; the final heat judges every final entry afresh.
- A dev grade on production, from your submission's receipt to its result: the transcript gate, then your program's two runs and the gate, each run in a sandbox of its own that takes about 2 seconds to start when few entries are being graded and 8 to 11 seconds at full load (up to 20 at the 90th percentile), your program's own time of up to 120 seconds a run, and about 10 seconds besides; then its 12 games against the house dashboards, 24 judge calls, or 40 calls when its rate reaches the line for the 2 extra rounds. No dev grade of this bounty has been timed yet.
- After the deadline every final entry is screened on the dev ladder rating it earned. The line sits at place 40: the larger of 4 times the last paid place and 32, among the rated entries' interval lows. An entry whose interval's high reaches the 40th-best low goes on; so does every house dashboard, and every entry with no complete dev rating on the final's judge and final artifact, which enters at the line (an entry whose dev record was cut short, or one judged by another judge). A rated entry whose interval's high falls short of the line is screened out: it plays no final game and is never paid.
- The shortlist plays 3 Swiss rounds, the first in an order drawn from the heat's seed and each later one pairing members of similar rate so far; then racing, in which an entry straddling a prize boundary plays more games up to its cap (48 games at the smallest drop, rising with the drop to at most 96), until its interval is within the race's epsilon of settled; then a playoff in which the first 12 of the shortlist by rate play every pair once, and 3 more games are played between the top two after it. A house dashboard never enters the playoff.

## Ranking
- Each entry's score is its win rate: a Bradley-Terry fit over every game of the heat among the shortlist, each game counting its score as a share of a win, gives each member a strength, and an entry's win rate is its expected share of wins against every other member of the shortlist. A screened-out entry keeps the numbers of its dev rating over no final game, and is ranked below every entry that played.
- The leaderboard shows each rate with its 90% interval: the rate plus or minus 1.6449 times its standard error, scaled by the heat's measured dispersion and phi_margin, kept within 0 and 1. Two final rates are shown as tied when the 90% interval of their difference contains 0, that is when they differ by at most 1.6449 times the square root of the sum of their squared standard errors. A provisional rate is never shown as tied. Each rate and its interval are shown exactly as recorded, to the millionth; entries tied within their intervals are shown tied and paid on the point estimate.
- Places go by the score, best first. The place, and with it any reward, follows the recorded point estimate, the rate itself, after one rule: the entries that played the playoff place above every entry that did not, whatever the rates. Within each of those two groups the higher rate places first. An exact tie on the win rate goes to the entry that took more of the playoff games between the tied entries, then to the smaller `dossier.json` (fewer bytes), then to the earlier receipt: the first of the tied entries submitted places first and the rest follow in the order they were submitted, each taking its own place's reward. The leaderboard's Size column shows each entry's dossier bytes. A displayed tie never moves a place.
- Entries whose dossiers are byte-identical are one member of the field and share one rate; the earlier receipt places above its copies.

## Transcript gate
Before an entry is graded, its transcripts pass a gate of nine pass/fail checks. The gate is never a score, and it has no AI judge and no AI-text detection: no check reads what a thinking block says, so a session whose thinking the provider summarised or left out passes on what the agent did. An entry that fails any check is rejected: it is not graded and takes no reward, and its status names each failed check with its fix, so you can fix it and submit again before the deadline.
- transcripts_present: at least one session is parsed from the upload.
- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (main.ts or main.py) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model, or to $0 where the record's session ran its model on your own machine (a local runtime or loopback server the session records, never a model's name or a self-export's stated provider), for this check alone, never the entry's build cost; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); a handed-over session need not carry usage, but the usage it does carry is held to the same rules; an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.

## Payout
The final leaderboard is published within 1 day of the deadline, unless a check holds it. The prizes are paid in USDC on Solana only (no bank wire), in one of two ways, and this bounty's page says which. When the bounty opens, its whole reward is locked in make.fun's escrow program on Solana if the escrow can be funded. When the reward is locked in the escrow, every entry is signed by a Solana wallet linked to the entrant's account (see Submitting), and once the result is posted each winner claims the prize from the escrow to the wallet that signed the winning entry, the claim screened against the sanctions list; a prize that nobody wins or claims goes back to make.fun's reward wallet after the escrow's claim window.

Otherwise the reward is paid from make.fun's reward wallet as follows, and an entry needs no signature. Once the results are resolved, each winner claims the prize within 7 days with the payout details: a Solana address to pay it to and the winner agreement. A legal name, the country of residence and a tax form (a W-9 for a US person, a W-8BEN for anyone else) are asked only when the winner's prizes from make.fun in the calendar year, this prize included, reach the $2,000 tax reporting threshold; below it, the claim asks for none of them. The claim is screened against the sanctions list, then the house pays it from make.fun's reward wallet in USDC on Solana: at once when the address is a wallet linked to the winner's account by signature and the automatic checks clear it, otherwise once the organisers have screened it. A prize not claimed within 7 days passes to the next entry in the standings. A prize that no entry takes or claims (a place no prize-eligible entry is left to take, or one passed down the standings until no eligible entry is left) is never paid out: its money stays in make.fun's reward wallet for later contests.

Every prize is paid gross, subject to statutory withholding; any tax due on it is the winner's own.
